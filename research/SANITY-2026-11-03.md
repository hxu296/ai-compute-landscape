## The remaining elephants in the room

1. **Installed and usable supply.** Owner shipment estimates and selected site power do not reveal a complete current online fleet, active utilization or inference-ready capacity. Hosts and clouds can describe the same racks. A global supply-demand balance is still unsupported.
2. **Comparable token demand.** Companies use overlapping scopes and usually omit input/output, cache, reasoning and modality accounting. Anthropic has no comparable public absolute total in the retained evidence; several other totals are historical or narrow subsets. There is no defensible additive global token total here.
3. **Financing and profitability.** Nominal multi-year deals do not establish current revenue, cash collected, credit exposure or project funding. A full balance-sheet/cash-flow and customer-concentration analysis remains absent. Cancellation rights, delivery conditions, leases and equity arrangements matter independently.
4. **The physical bottlenecks.** HBM, advanced packaging, networking, cooling, interconnection and commissioning can each constrain delivered useful compute. The current ledger has selected evidence, not a complete reconciliation from chip production through powered racks. MTIA and domestic Chinese accelerators also lack comparable owner-level counts.
5. **Cost of useful work.** The capacity calculator is a conditional extrapolation from particular coding traces and serving configurations. Model quality, workload mix, latency targets, cache reuse and reliability prevent treating its rack ratio as a universal cost advantage. Negotiated rates and matched hardware resale transactions are also incomplete.

These gaps are now explicit research priorities in [the coverage queue](GAPS.md). They should guide the next data pass, not be filled with numerical guesses.
