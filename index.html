The landscape

How the companies connect

Facility owners lease powered space to cloud operators. Clouds supply AI labs and other clouds. Follow the disclosed links to see who depends on whom.

Swipe across the diagram to follow facilities → clouds → buyers, or choose a company above.

Equal-width links show selected public agreements, including unpriced ones. Motion indicates direction, not volume. Small numbers count counterparties. A connected path does not allocate particular racks or megawatts through a reseller. Undisclosed relationships stay absent.

Most numbers in this business are announced rather than measured. Contract terms can include floors or ceilings, a GPU count is often worked out from megawatts, and a hyperscaler's fleet is somebody else's model. So every figure here carries its source and a tag distinguishing reporting, calculation, and estimation.

How to read the tags
R
Reported by a company, filing, or named press source; this tag is not independent verification.
D
Derived from reported numbers by a stated formula, such as value ÷ term ÷ GPUs.
E
Estimated by an analyst or inferred from indirect evidence. Source ranges are retained where available.

Why Google’s daily and monthly figures differ

Google’s May 2026 figure of over 3.2 quadrillion tokens/month covers its surfaces broadly (over 106.7T/day using a 30-day month). The October disclosure of nearly 32B/minute covers first-party model APIs (nearly 46.08T/day as a rate equivalent). The old 3T/day series covers internal developer tools. These are overlapping scopes from different dates; none should be substituted for another. May disclosure · October disclosure · Internal-tools disclosure.

Build-out

Compute being built vs compute needed, ex-China

Bars: GB300 NVL72 rack-equivalents (182 H100e each) owned by covered companies at end-2025 plus additions at Epoch-tracked sites. Marker: racks needed for ex-China tokens at the chosen growth, derate and serving share. The verdict turns on the derate: how far real fleets run below benchmark efficiency, including utilization (70% usable alone is 1.4×).

Serving share is one lab’s compute spend split (Anthropic’s filing implies 26–36% serving), not GPU-hours. Supply excludes ByteDance, Huawei and Alibaba to match ex-China demand. Racks are counted by GPUs (needed GPUs ÷ 72) to compare with supply; the capacity calculator below counts whole racks, which leaves GPUs unused when a serving group does not divide 72 (about 1.3× more racks for V4-Pro, 1.8× for Kimi). 2028 additions are mostly Rubin, which Epoch counts at 8.84 H100e by 8-bit FLOPs; decode is bandwidth-bound and Rubin has about 4–6.6× H100’s bandwidth, so the bandwidth options count the Rubin part 1.3–2× lower. Rubin is about 76% of the H100e in Epoch’s 2028-dated chip entries (ex-China), so 2028 additions scale by 0.82 or 0.62; GB300 and other chips keep their FLOP count. Tracked sites cover frontier campuses only; planned dates can slip, most of all Oracle’s 2028 Stargate sites. Estimates (E).

Capacity scenario

If this traffic ran on open models

Racks needed if the estimated November 2026 traffic ran on one open model. One measured GB200/GB300 NVL72 configuration per model: the highest output throughput per GPU above 50 tokens/s per user.

GLM-5.3 has no NVL72 run, so GLM-5.2 is shown. Physical GPUs per group are checked against each run’s job topology. A configuration comparison, not an equal-quality ranking.

“Usable capacity” reserves room for downtime, peaks and other losses relative to the benchmark. At 70%, 30% is reserved; it is not a measured GPU utilization rate. Per-user speeds below are reciprocals of P90 response inter-token latency.

Enter a positive traffic volume and nonnegative finite cost and power assumptions.

Tokens are not all the same work

Cache hits apply only to input tokens. This control changes the token composition above; it does not change the rack estimate. The runs do not isolate uncached prefill capacity, so they cannot calibrate a cache-hit-to-rack curve. Rack estimates retain the chosen benchmark’s context and cache behavior.

Assumptions, packing and source checks

Defaults: SemiAnalysis’s GB300 NVL72 page, retrieved November 3, 2026: $2.31 per chip-hour owning at large hyperscaler volume, 2.12 kW all-in power per chip (host, networking and cooling share). Annual cost = all physical GPUs × hourly cost × 8,760. Neither figure is a purchase quote.

Method: group equivalents = max(input tokens/day ÷ 86,400 ÷ group input TPS, output tokens/day ÷ 86,400 ÷ group output TPS) ÷ usable capacity. Round up to groups, then to whole racks. This is a fixed-rate extrapolation from coding-trace replay. A different input/output mix, context length, cache distribution or serving stack can change the attainable rates. Full-rack packing was not measured.

Benchmark scope: AgentX variable-length coding traces, prefix caching and speculative decoding. Runs use different engines, parallel layouts and cache setups; this is a configuration comparison, not a controlled model-only comparison. Input throughput counts reused context as well as fresh input.

Why not use the headline? The run pages’ 100-token/s figures interpolate total logical throughput. They are not output throughput. Pro’s overview also applies a decode-share factor after the raw producer has already divided by all allocated GPUs. This calculator uses the raw absolute input and output rates and explicit physical groups instead. Producer · Overview transformation.

Source metadata: API precision labels are reported as given and do not establish uniform arithmetic precision. The selected runs, full raw rows, job topologies and response hashes are in the expanded snapshot; the earlier GB200 selections remain in the original snapshot.

The historical Fireworks Pro 0813 serverless tariff is not used as current revenue: Fireworks says that endpoint was retired from serverless. Current GPU ownership cost and hypothetical API billing are different quantities.
