Who supplies AI compute, at what price, and for what: November 2026

GPU capacity now comes from three kinds of supplier: the hyperscalers (AWS, Microsoft, Google, Oracle, plus Meta and xAI building for themselves), neoclouds that rent GPUs (CoreWeave, Nebius, Lambda, Crusoe and others), and power hosts that build the halls and lease them to both. This page puts their fleets, contracts, prices and growth side by side. Every number links to its source and carries a tag: R reported by the company or a filing, D derived by a stated formula, E estimated from indirect evidence.

Who supplies it

No hyperscaler discloses how many accelerators it owns, so the compute view uses Epoch AI's estimates. Neoclouds and hosts disclose power far more often than GPUs, so the power view compares them in megawatts. Hosts carry MW only: the GPUs inside belong to their tenants.

The big contracts

Who has signed to buy capacity from whom. Band width is the disclosed contract value. Hover for terms, click a buyer or supplier to see only its deals. Framework and equity deals (such as Stargate's $500B plan) are left out of the flows; building leases (a host renting powered halls to a neocloud or hyperscaler) and chip purchases have their own views, because the same capacity often appears in a lease and again in the compute deal built on it.

Implied prices: $/GPU-hour only when the deal gives a GPU count; otherwise an all-in $/kW-month from the MW (comparable across chip generations) and, for GPU deals, an estimated $/GPU-hour range at pre-Rubin watts that is a floor for 2027+ deliveries. Leases of powered buildings are $/kW-month and do not include chips.
