Who supplies AI compute, at what price, and for what

Three kinds of company now sell GPU capacity: the hyperscalers, the neoclouds that rent GPUs, and the power hosts that build the halls. This guide lines up their fleets, contracts, prices and growth, one question at a time.

The landscape

Halls, clouds and their customers

Facility owners lease powered space to cloud operators. Clouds supply AI labs and other clouds. Follow the disclosed links to see who depends on whom.

Swipe across the diagram to follow facilities → clouds → buyers, or choose a company above.

Equal-width links show selected public agreements, including unpriced ones. Motion indicates direction, not volume. Small numbers count counterparties. A connected path does not allocate particular racks or megawatts through a reseller. Undisclosed relationships stay absent.

Most numbers in this business are announced rather than measured. Contract terms can include floors or ceilings, a GPU count is often worked out from megawatts, and a hyperscaler's fleet is somebody else's model. So every figure here carries its source and a tag distinguishing reporting, calculation, and estimation.

How to read the tags
R
Reported by a company, filing, or named press source; this tag is not independent verification.
D
Derived from reported numbers by a stated formula, such as value ÷ term ÷ GPUs.
E
Estimated by an analyst or inferred from indirect evidence. Source ranges are retained where available.

Hover over any mark for its terms and source. Click a buyer or supplier in the contract flow to see only its deals. Tables sort when you click a column. A source-note marker opens the two dated claims: “source conflict” means unresolved disagreement, “different bases” means the definitions differ, and “later evidence” identifies dated updates. Source-date warnings remain separate from verified revisions.

01Fleets

Accelerator fleets and power capacity

No hyperscaler says how many accelerators it owns. The best public estimate is Epoch AI's model of chip deliveries, which converts every chip into H100-equivalents so that a TPU and a GPU can share an axis. Neoclouds and hosts rarely disclose GPU counts either, but they do disclose power, so the second view compares them in megawatts, including clearly labelled partial AI-site estimates for the hyperscalers.

02Contracts

Compute contracts and commercial relationships

A compute contract is a promise to pay for capacity over several years. The flow below runs from buyer to supplier, and the default view shows relationships even when prices are undisclosed. Switch to disclosed dollars to size the bands by value. Framework deals such as Stargate's $500B plan are left out, because they commit no one to a price.

Building leases get their own view. A host that rents a powered hall to Fluidstack, which then sells the same capacity to Anthropic, would otherwise be counted twice.

How implied prices are worked out

$/GPU-hour is shown only when a deal gives a GPU count. Otherwise the page gives an all-in $/kW-month from the megawatts, which stays comparable across chip generations. For GPU deals it adds an estimated $/GPU-hour range at pre-Rubin power draw, which is a floor for 2027 and later deliveries. Leases of powered buildings are priced per kW-month and include no chips.

04Prices

Rental prices and commitment terms

Compare market benchmarks first, then published provider rates by commitment term. A hyperscaler’s one-year retail reservation is not a negotiated neocloud contract. TPU and Trainium rates use physical chip-hours; price alone does not compare model throughput.

Market price movements

Same-series changes from public benchmark data, calculated over calendar windows. Hover a change for its exact start and end dates. A rental index is not a GPU resale price.

H100 one-year rental eased about 3.1% over the last 30 days while B200/B300 one-year indices rose. SemiAnalysis’s B200 composite fell about 6.3% in the last week but remains higher over 30 and 90 days. These observations do not show a broad collapse across GPU markets.
