How demand is calculated

More assumptions, method and limits

Read it as: a small inference share is not idle compute. Rental indices are up over 90 days (SemiAnalysis H100 and B200, early November 2026; mixed over 30 days) and labs buy short-term compute at a premium, so the rest is in use: training (labs choose its size, it does not scale with tokens), traffic we cannot count (the revenue check says our tokens run ≈1.6–2× low), recommender and ads workloads in Meta’s and Google’s fleets, and chips delivered but not yet powered. When inference passes labs’ serving share, inference and training compete for the same compute.

Software efficiency check: if Anthropic’s list prices for its main models stayed flat through 2026, a constant realized price is fair and the cost-of-revenue check is weak evidence for 1×, not none. We have not yet checked the 2026 price history.

Method: inference demand = ex-China logical tokens/day, grown monthly from the projection date. It is priced, not benchmarked: GPUs needed = tokens × serving cost per token ÷ price per GPU-hour. Serving cost per token comes from Anthropic’s filing: cost of revenue $4.812B in January–September 2026 (“substantially all” customer-serving compute) ÷ its ≈123T/day of consumption-billed tokens over those months = ≈$0.143 per million tokens. This bundles utilization, model size, chip mix and overheads as they actually occurred at one large lab. It overstates cost per token because subscription traffic is not in the token count, and Anthropic’s models are likely heavier than average; cheaper-to-serve traffic elsewhere would need fewer GPUs. The note under the chart shows how many times the GPUs the DeepSeek-V4-Pro benchmark would need this implies.

        How to read this chart

        Built from the same contract rows as Table 2 (non-excluded, announced from the selected year). The six largest buyers are named; everyone else is one grey “Other buyers” row and undisclosed counterparties one “Undisclosed buyers” row; beyond the 14 largest clouds and 10 largest landlords the rest are grouped. A company that both buys and sells (Oracle, CoreWeave) is drawn once per side it acts on; its other role is named in the hover. Buyer → cloud width is disclosed nominal value; cloud → landlord width is contracted MW; each side has its own scale. Dashed links have no disclosed size. A row that its source marks as the same underlying deal as another (OpenAI–Oracle, 4.5 GW, 2025-07-22 and 2025-09-10) is counted once in MW. “Disclosed landlords” are the listed hosts and builders in the deal data (miners, Crusoe and others); hyperscalers’ own campuses and wholesale developers such as QTS, Vantage and Stack are not in the deal data, so this column is not the whole landlord side. Add chip-supply orders to include NVIDIA, AMD and other hardware deals. Framework plans such as Stargate’s $500B are excluded: they commit no one to a price. Building leases are kept apart from compute services, so the same capacity is not counted twice. “Firm” means a stated amount that is not “up to”, conditional, cancellable or potential. A connected path does not allocate particular racks or megawatts through a reseller. Amounts retain their conditions and can overlap.

        How implied prices are worked out

        No contract here gives a GPU count, so every $/GPU-hour is an estimate from the all-in $/kW-month, which stays comparable across chip generations: $/kW-month × kW per GPU ÷ 730 hours. It is shown only for NVIDIA and AMD GPU deals, not Trainium, TPU or Cerebras capacity.         How to read this chart

        Each bar assumes today’s estimated traffic ran on one open model, at that model’s best measured throughput on GB300 NVL72 racks (above 50 tokens/s per user). A configuration comparison, not an equal-quality ranking. MW is facility power at the all-in kW per GPU set below (default 2.12 kW, SemiAnalysis’s GB300-class figure).

        Enter a positive traffic volume and nonnegative finite cost and power assumptions.

        Per-model operating point, racks and GPUs

        Tokens are not all the same work

        Cache hits change the token mix above, not the rack estimate: the benchmarks do not isolate uncached prefill, so racks keep each run’s own cache behavior.

        Assumptions, packing and source checks

        GLM-5.3 has no NVL72 run, so GLM-5.2 is shown. Physical GPUs per group are checked against each run’s job topology. Usable capacity reserves headroom for downtime and peaks (not a measured utilization). Speeds are 1 ÷ P90 inter-token latency.

        Defaults: SemiAnalysis’s GB300 NVL72 page, retrieved November 3, 2026: $2.31 per chip-hour owning at large hyperscaler volume, 2.12 kW all-in power per chip (host, networking and cooling share). Annual cost = all physical GPUs × hourly cost × 8,760. Neither figure is a purchase quote.

        Method: group equivalents = max(input tokens/day ÷ 86,400 ÷ group input TPS, output tokens/day ÷ 86,400 ÷ group output TPS) ÷ usable capacity. Round up to groups, then to whole racks. This is a fixed-rate extrapolation from coding-trace replay. A different input/output mix, context length, cache distribution or serving stack can change the attainable rates. Full-rack packing was not measured.

        Benchmark scope: AgentX variable-length coding traces, prefix caching and speculative decoding. Runs use different engines, parallel layouts and cache setups; this is a configuration comparison, not a controlled model-only comparison. Input throughput counts reused context as well as fresh input.

        Why not use the headline? The run pages’ 100-token/s figures interpolate total logical throughput. They are not output throughput. Pro’s overview also applies a decode-share factor after the raw producer has already divided by all allocated GPUs. This calculator uses the raw absolute input and output rates and explicit physical groups instead. Producer · Overview transformation.

        Source metadata: API precision labels are reported as given and do not establish uniform arithmetic precision. The selected runs, full raw rows, job topologies and response hashes are in the expanded snapshot; the earlier GB200 selections remain in the original snapshot.

        The historical Fireworks Pro 0813 serverless tariff is not used as current revenue: Fireworks says that endpoint was retired from serverless. Current GPU ownership cost and hypothetical API billing are different quantities.
